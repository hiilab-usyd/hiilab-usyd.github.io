Changing the way someone walks is a common rehabilitation goal after injury, surgery or neurological conditions, but it usually depends on clinic sessions with a therapist watching closely. Outside the clinic, people get little feedback on whether they are walking the way they should.

This project develops a closed-loop wearable system that combines augmented reality glasses with body-worn motion sensors to guide walking in everyday settings. The system detects how a person moves, compares it with their target gait, and shows subtle, personalised cues in their view, such as a prompt to take a slightly longer step or keep a steady pace. As the person adapts, it tracks progress and adjusts the cues. The project builds on our earlier work on human kinematics and movement, and is in early development with clinical partnerships under discussion.
