Online health information can help people understand their health concerns and prepare for medical appointments. For some, though, searching becomes excessive and repetitive, and comes with growing health anxiety and distress. This pattern is central to cyberchondria. Older migrants may be particularly exposed: language barriers, limited eHealth literacy and difficulty getting professional clarification can make health-related uncertainty harder to resolve, yet how these challenges relate to cyberchondria remains unclear.

This project combines a scoping review, following PRISMA-ScR guidelines, with a design synthesis for robot-based health-information support. None of the 132 sources included in the review directly examined cyberchondria or problematic searching among older migrants, and evidence on robot-based support was limited. Across related populations, however, studies reported difficulties understanding health information, judging its credibility and obtaining professional clarification, alongside persistent uncertainty, escalating concern and information avoidance.

From these findings we developed preliminary design considerations for robot-based support that helps people manage health-information uncertainty: clarifying health concerns, appraising the credibility of information, regulating how much information people take in and how long they keep searching, and escalating to professional care when needed. The next step is to refine and evaluate these considerations with older migrants themselves.
