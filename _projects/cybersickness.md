Virtual reality is moving into training, therapy and everyday work, yet many users still experience cybersickness: nausea, disorientation and discomfort during or after immersion. Most research measures it with questionnaires after the fact. This project asks what the body itself reveals.

We study how VR affects posture and movement, using motion capture to track body kinematics and centre of mass while people are immersed. Our work shows that changes in how people move and stabilise themselves carry signatures of cybersickness, and we combine these measures with machine learning to understand and predict when users are becoming unwell. The long-term goal is safer immersive systems that detect discomfort and adapt before it escalates.
